A master's ticket is the certificate to command a vessel. This track works toward the ham-radio
equivalent: the knowledge behind an amateur radio licence exam. It shares Track A's foundation, then
adds the RF-analysis spine and the ham-specific material.

> **This track prepares you for an amateur licence exam. Finishing it is not a licence** — for that
> you pass a **VEC** exam (the volunteer-examiner licence test). A licence lets *you* transmit on
> suitable amateur radios; it does not give Fancy a new transmitter (its SDR is receive-only and its
> SX1262 transmits only on 902–928 MHz), and it does not change what Fancy may legally do on its own.

**Intended sequence** (dependency-ordered): **M0 · Orientation and the rules** → **M1 · Power and
rails** → **M1b · Who owns what** → **M2 · dB, antennas and link budget** → **M5 · SDR foundations
and capture discipline** → **M8 · Identifying signals** → **M10 · Ham bands, APRS
(Automatic Packet Reporting System) and licensing**, with **M9 · Satellite passes** optional (+3 h).
**M3** (from Track A) is recommended before M10 for its GNSS/timing grounding. Every module ends with
a lab on Fancy, checked against live readings.

**Available today** (~15.5 h): **M0, M1, M1b** (the same three foundation modules that open Track A),
**M2 · dB, antennas and link budget**, **M5 · SDR foundations and capture discipline**,
**M8 · Identifying signals**, **M10 · Ham bands, APRS and licensing** — the ham-specific module that
completes the core spine, ending in a live APRS decode — and the optional **M9 · Satellite passes**
(+3 h), shared with Track B. The whole track is now built end to end; finishing it prepares you for the
exam, and is not itself a licence.
